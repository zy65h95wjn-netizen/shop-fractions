# One minimal front-facing Holly revision

**Proposed artwork, not a completed or validated asset.** The image editor returned no candidate. This specification requests one localized retouch of the existing portrait, followed by a test with the existing r6 renderer. It does not request a new animation rig.

Deliver one flattened RGB portrait registered to the original **720×1280** composition, as a separate experimental file. Preserve the original JPEG. Keep face, expression, copper hair color, ears, hat, front-facing stance, body proportions, arm positions, green velvet/gold lacing/white fur outfit identity, red skirt, stockings, lighting and snowy background. Keep the face/head and lower body unchanged wherever possible. Do not solve clearance by changing anatomy, rotating the character or enlarging the canvas subject.

Make these three connected changes:

1. **Clear the hair from the moving garment boundaries.** Sweep the lower lengths behind both shoulders while retaining face framing and hair volume above them. Concentrate the retouch around the left outer skin/fur/cup corridor (approximately x=190–285, y=410–645) and right corridor (x=545–630, y=420–645). Remove loose foreground strands crossing the upper-cup boundary and paint the small revealed shoulder/arm/garment areas naturally. Retain the original shoulder and arm shapes. Do not clone fur or stretch existing hair into those areas.
2. **Give the moving fur and upper cups actual garment support inside the body outline.** Raise or widen the plain green velvet side panels slightly at the underarms. Let the fur ends and upper-cup seam lie inside those panels, initially aiming for 35–45 original pixels between the moving detail and the stationary arm/background outline. As rough guides, investigate the left fur end around x=245–265 and the right around x=560–575 at the existing upper-cup heights; these are guides, not requirements to reshape the body. Keep those narrow side panels free of crossing hair, laces, heavy embroidery and straight boning. Keep soft cloth grain and existing shading. The moving trim must remain clearly mobile within the support; relocating it into a zero-motion band is not an acceptable fix.
3. **Clarify the lower attachment.** Use a continuous, softly curved seam where each upper cup joins the fixed lower bodice, roughly y=570–630. Avoid an isolated rigid scalloped border or abrupt vertical gold lines across the strongest transition. Preserve the central lacing and lower corset, especially the fixed center corridor around x=374–440. Keep the cup's apparent volume and the outfit's silhouette; do not flatten the upper garment to disguise strain.

The exact edit should follow the photographed garment, not a rectangular patch. Maintain naturally continuous light, material grain and shadows. No duplicated strands, fur spikes, halos, smeared arm texture or invented gaps. The [r6 support overlay](evidence/geometry-holly.jpg) indicates where the existing map is strongest; the [failed broader trial](evidence/geometry-holly-trial.jpg) shows the neighboring material that must be kept out of the moving area.

Why this is the smallest proposal: hair clearance addresses one overlap conflict; plain side panels address the independent arm/background conflict; the lower seam addresses the independent attachment conflict. Hair removal by itself cannot resolve all three. The pose, face, body design and most of the original photograph can be retained. The 35–45-pixel margin is an initial design allowance, not a measured sufficient minimum.

Test the flattened candidate first with the **original r6 renderer and unchanged spring states**, then only modest support calibration if its photographed boundaries require it. Use the same left/right impact, peak, return, rebound and settled states in this review on WebGL and Canvas. Confirm the skin, full visible fur trim and upper cups move cohesively while side support, hair, arms and scenery stay believable. Compare full composition as well as native close-ups. Reject frozen fur ends, excessive movement reduction, distorted neighbors, cup flattening or a seam that visibly disconnects.

If that one candidate still fails, report which material boundary fails and whether the art can be corrected locally. Do not infer that a different pose, further reconstruction layers or another animation architecture is necessary without evidence and approval. Do not replace Holly's approved portrait until human visual acceptance.
